One index, three layers.

adamthede.com links to almost nothing it publishes. This puts every place under the domain, and four elsewhere, in one list. It opens as a drawer from the top of the home page, stands at /index/ as a plain page, and closes the home page as a footer list. Nothing here is built. Pick drawer A or B, or redirect it.

What A and B do differently

  1. A is a printed index. Four groups run down columns, and every name, line and address shows at once with nothing to point at.
  2. B is a map. Every place is the same tile in one grid, and the group names sit in the grid as labels.
  3. B draws the sign-in wall. A double rule runs the full width, open places sit above it and family pages below. In A the family group carries the same lock and line in its own column.
  4. B has a readout. A strip on the drawer's bottom edge, the home page's meter idiom, shows the full address of the tile you point at, who can open it and where it opens.
  5. B pairs records with family editions. Point at A Viewing Life and its family edition lights up, joined by a dashed line through the wall. Two more pairs work the same way. A says this in each family entry's line instead.
  6. A is cheaper and calmer. It fits a 1280 by 800 window with a short scroll and needs no script beyond open and close. B needs about 60 more lines of script, and on a desktop its addresses show only in the readout.

Decisions for you, with a recommendation each

  1. Which drawer. Recommend A. It is an index first and shows everything at once. B is the more striking object, but you have to point at a tile to learn its address.
  2. The plain page's address. Recommend /index/, built from content/places.md with url /index/. A scratch build confirmed it sits beside the home page without a clash.
  3. Phone corner chips. Recommend folding the business chip into the index on phones under 560px, so the trigger fits between résumé and night.
  4. Two letters pages. The family may call both of these "the Big Letter". The labels split them as "The Big Letter" (my monthly letter) and "The letters archive". Recommend keeping them as drafted unless the family uses other names.
  5. The handwritten line. "No code? Ask me to add your email." Recommend keeping it. An email that is not on the Access list never gets a code, so this is the one thing a relative needs to know.
  6. The three family editions. I inferred the pairs from matching titles and sources: the viewing diary and A Viewing Life, the book record and The Reading Life, the article archive and The Week in Reading. Confirm or correct them.
